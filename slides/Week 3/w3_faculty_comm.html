Elevator pitch

  • 1-2 minute pitch including:
    • who you are
    • your background
    • your project idea
    • projections for the future (how project will help your academic career)
    • OR a question for the person

  • Jonathan A. Pedroza, but you can call me JP
  • I am postdoctoral researcher in data science education
  • My background is in Prevention Science where
  • Interest in use of diagnostic classification models

Reaching out to advisors

  • What worries you most about reaching out to advisors?
    • They are an expert in their field
    • They hold a position of power over me
    • What if they think my idea is stupid?
    • I’m wasting their time and they are busy
    • What if they don’t respond?
  • What other worries do you have?

Reaching out to advisors

  • They are an expert in their field
    • They are an expert ONLY in their field
    • This expertise in their field means they probably struggle with other skills

Reaching out to advisors

  • They hold a position of power over me
    • Power is relative
    • They hold power over you in this specific field
    • If an advisor is getting food and they are being rude
      • Are they still in power?
  • I’ve heard faculty say some real questionable things
    • Like my reaction is “how?”
  • You’re probably more normal than your advisor
    • You hold that power

Reaching out to advisors

  • What if they think my idea is stupid?
    • Rethink if your research interests match their research
    • Otherwise, this is a red flag
    • Advisors should be able to shift your interests to be realistic and align with their work if the fit is somewhat there
  • If the fit is not there, don’t force the relationship to work

Reaching out to advisors

  • I’m wasting their time and they are busy
    • If an advisor doesn’t have time for you that is okay
    • You find a new advisor
    • Also, you’re not wasting their time
    • Advisors are academics because they love advancing research
      • If interests align, most will show interest

Reaching out to advisors

  • What if they don’t respond?
    • They may not
    • Check back in after a week with follow-up email
    • If no response, look for a new advisor

Points about advisors

  • Remember, all potential advisors are people
  • Don’t choose advisors based on the name
    • Pick based on fit with their research
    • Pick based on faculty’s level of commitment/availability
    • Don’t bad mouth people (i.e., advisors)
  • Both interested in similar topics
    • Don’t be afraid to be excited
    • Excited is great
  • At the end of the day, you’re both interested in the same research topic

This is a relationship

  • This is academic dating
    • You may get rejected
  • Just like romantic relationships
    • You have a voice
  • Toxic relationships
    • Would you date someone famous and toxic?

  • Just like romantic relationships, you have a voice in the relationship

How to reach most faculty

  • Emails
  • Office hours
  • Meeting at departmental social event
  • If you don’t know someone AT ALL
    • Don’t give pitch if you see them in public
    • If you took a class with them, say hi, then you can email them

Focal points of email

  • There’s a lot that goes into it
    • Try to make it short
  • NEVER WRITE A DRAFT IN THE EMAIL
  • If reaching out to multiple advisors
    • Don’t copy + paste without some sort of system
  • Reread your email
  • Actually read about their research AND AT LEAST 1 PUBLICATION

What to read in their articles

  • Find their Google Scholar
    • Check this against their faculty/lab website
    • Choose the article that sounds interesting to you
  • Find something that matches to your idea

Meeting with your (potential) advisor

  • Be prepared when you meet with your advisor
    • Come with agenda
    • Recurring meetings (weekly vs biweekly)
      • Send email with meeting notes
    • Cancel if you don’t have updates
      • Do this sparingly
  • Know when it is your time to leave
    • When agenda is complete, check in on anything else lingering

Photo by Ketut Subiyanto

  • BE THERE ON TIME, OR EARLY

  • Negative bias (the focus on everything bad)

  • Biweekly meaning every other week, not twice a week (DON”T DO THAT)

Being prepared for your meeting

  • What goes into your agenda
    • PhD, EdD, or other doctorate = Refer to them as Dr. always
    • Be human
      • Little conversation
    • Explain your advisor idea OR
    • Ask about any project ideas the advisor has
      • What would they be willing to help you build an independent project around
    • What is needed from you (trainings)
    • Ask if they have seminal/crucial research that you should read
    • Your assignments (getting approval of RQs & agreement on timeline)
  • I’m the exception. Don’t call me Dr.

“Small talk” for meeting with advisor

  • “How is your semester going?”
  • “Thanks for making time to have me here”
  • “I like your office”
    • “Oh that is a cool ______ {notice something in their office}”
  • Chat about anything
    • Advisors are human
    • You’re gonna have a relationship with them

Be prepared to change

  • NEVER fall in love with your research idea
    • Destined for failure
  • Your idea will adapt from your original project
  • It could adapt as you read more literature
  • It may adapt because you don’t have the capabilities to answer RQs
    • Data doesn’t answer the question
    • You don’t have the expertise to analyze it
    • Many other reasons
  • Be realistic and kind to yourself
